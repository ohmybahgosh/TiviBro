Phone setup

Put the remote down. Set it up from your phone.

Typing a server address, username and password with arrow keys is nobody's idea of fun. TiviBro's first screen shows a QR code instead.

  1. 1

    Open TiviBro

    A QR code waits beside the login form. The address and password are under it, for a computer.

  2. 2

    Scan it with your phone's camera

    TiviBro's editor opens on your phone, already signed in. Add your playlist, or restore a backup from another TV.

  3. 3

    Paste your login

    Xtream Codes or M3U, with a real keyboard. The TV tests it before saving, loads your channels and starts the set-up tour on its own.

  4. 4

    Keep tidying, any time

    The same editor, on your phone or computer, renames, hides, sorts and cleans up thousands of channels while the TV follows.

First start

Your playlist, tidied before the first show.

Connect, and TiviBro shows what your list holds, then walks you through it with the remote. It only asks about what your playlist has, keeps a running count, and changes nothing until Apply.

  • Keep the countries you watch, read from any naming style.
  • Clean names, with your own channels shown before and after.
  • East or West feeds, and your city's local stations in every local group.
  • Sports and pay-per-view, duplicates, divider groups, logos and colours.

Channel logos

Every channel gets its logo.

Providers leave logos out, or send ones that no longer load. The logo library fills them in from two open projects, iptv-org and tv-logos.

  • Matched by guide id, cleaned name and country, and call sign for local stations.
  • Fill in only what's missing, or use one matching style for every channel.
  • A provider logo that fails to load is swapped for the library's.
  • TiviBro hosts no images: each logo loads from its own project.

Pay-per-view and sports passes

The game is in the name. Now it's in the guide.

Providers often skip the guide for pay-per-view, NFL, NBA, NHL and college passes, and write the whole listing into the channel name instead:

NCAAB 001 | NORTH FLORIDA @ CHARLESTON SOUTHERN (M) | 12.18 11:00 AM | ESPN+NCAAB 001 · North Florida @ Charleston Southern, 11:00 AM

  • The guide says Starts 7:30 PM: Kings vs Pelicans, then shows the game at its real time.
  • Events by time lists every upcoming game and fight, soonest first.
  • ET, PT, UK and CET times are converted to your TV's time, daylight saving included.

The web editor

Clean up 10,000 channels from your phone or computer.

Pick Edit on phone in the TV's side menu and scan the QR code, or open the address it shows on a computer. Changes are drafts until you press Send to TV.

  • Hide, sort, rename, copy and move channels into any group.
  • Smart clean-up: your countries, your city's locals, East or West feeds, duplicates, divider and empty groups.
  • Smart match finds the right guide for channels without one.
  • Drafts are saved on the TV within seconds, with a version history.
  • Logins never leave the TV; the editor only sees server names.

Backups

New TV? Everything back in a minute.

One file holds every playlist with its login, your groups, favorites, names, guide matches and settings, locked with your passphrase (AES-256-GCM) before it leaves the TV. On a brand-new install, scan the QR code on the very first screen and restore it from your phone.

Install

On your TV in a few minutes.

TiviBro installs like any homebrew app. Pick the way that suits your TV.

The easiest way on a rooted TV, with updates from the TV itself.

  1. Open the Homebrew Channel, go to Settings, and choose Add repository.
  2. Enter this address:
    https://tivibro.app/repo
  3. TiviBro appears in the app list. Choose Install. New versions show up as Update, and inside TiviBro under Settings, Updates and about.
  4. Open TiviBro and scan the QR code on the first screen with your phone to add your playlist there.

Want every test build as soon as it's out? Add https://tivibro.app/testing instead.

Switch on SSH or Telnet in the Homebrew Channel, log in as root, and run:

curl -fsSL https://tivibro.app/install | sh

It downloads the newest build, checks its SHA-256, installs it and starts TiviBro. Run it again any time to update; your settings are kept.

No root? Use LG's Developer Mode and the webOS CLI.

  1. Put your TV in Developer Mode.
  2. Download the .ipk from the latest release.
  3. Install it with ares-install -d yourTV app.tivibro.tv_*.ipk, or build from source with npm run build and npm run install:tv.

TiviBro is in active development: these are test builds. Keep a backup before updating, and report bugs in Issues.

Questions

Good to know.

Does TiviBro come with channels?

No. TiviBro is a player. It plays your own subscription from your provider, as an Xtream Codes login or an M3U playlist with an XMLTV guide.

Which TVs does it run on?

LG TVs with webOS 4 (2018) and newer. It is built and tuned for webOS 25 and tested on the browser and Node engines of older webOS versions.

Do I have to type my login with the remote?

No. Scan the QR code on the first screen with your phone and type it there, or restore a backup from another TV.

What's the "network helper"?

A part of TiviBro that runs in the background on the TV and fetches your channels, guide and streams for the app, because webOS won't let an app talk to most IPTV servers directly. It only talks to your own provider. Leave Connection on Automatic.

Is it really free?

Yes. It's MIT licensed open source, with no accounts, ads or paid tiers, and the app on your TV never tracks you.

Are my logins safe?

Logins stay on the TV. The web editor only ever sees server names, it needs the password shown on the TV, and backups are always encrypted with a passphrase you choose.

Something isn't working. How do I report it?

On the TV, open Settings > Diagnostics > Report a problem (or Report a problem on the first screen) and scan the QR code. Your phone opens a short form; the TV adds its version, model and log with logins and server names taken out, and GitHub's bug form opens filled in. Attach the log file it saves and send.

Where's the full manual?

The user guide covers every remote button, every screen and every setting, with videos and screenshots.
