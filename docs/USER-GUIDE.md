## On the TV

### First start

A brand-new TiviBro opens on **Add your playlist**.  You have two ways in:

- **Scan the QR code with your phone.**  It sits beside the form and opens the web editor on your phone, already signed in: type your login there with a real keyboard, or restore a backup from another TV.  The address and password are under the code, for a computer on the same network.  While you work there, a big banner along the bottom of the TV shows what it receives ("Checking the login and loading the channels…", then how many channels it added, or why it didn't work).  Any button on the TV remote clears it.  The TV moves on by itself when you finish, then shows the [set-up tour](#the-set-up-tour).  Nothing starts playing on its own.  (**Set up or restore from a phone or computer** shows the same code on a screen of its own.)
- **Type it on the TV.**  The type starts on **Full link**: paste the whole link your provider sent (the long one with username and password in it, often with get.php) and TiviBro says what it found, then fills in the Xtream Codes login when you press **Connect**.  Or pick Xtream Codes or M3U and fill in the server, username and password (or the M3U address) yourself.  A guide address is optional.  **Test connection** checks everything before you save; **Connect** saves and loads your channels.

### The set-up tour

Right after a new playlist connects, TiviBro shows what it has and walks you through making it yours, one screen at a time with the remote.  It only asks about what your playlist actually has, so a list with no local stations never shows the city step.  Nothing changes until the last screen, and a restore point is saved first, so the web editor can put everything back.

The first screen counts your channels, groups, countries, event channels, East and West copies, local cities, extra copies and whether there are movies and shows.  Then:

- **Tidy up here, step by step** goes through the screens below.  The chip in the top corner keeps a running count ("Keeps 3,632 of 9,808 channels").
- **Tidy up on a phone or computer** shows a QR code.  Scan it with your phone's camera and the web editor opens on [Smart clean-up](#smart-clean-up), already signed in.  The address and password are there too, for a computer.
- **Skip, start watching** changes nothing.

| Screen | What you pick |
|---|---|
| **Countries** | Each country and region in the list, biggest first, with how many channels it has.  The TV's own country is ticked; **Keep every country** and **Keep only** the biggest are one press.  Groups with no country (sports, events, 24/7) are kept unless you say otherwise. |
| **Channel names** | Name clean-up on or off, group names too, and every rule, with your own channels shown before and after. |
| **East or West?** | Keep East feeds, West feeds or both, and whether to hide groups named West (or East).  Examples from your list. |
| **Your local channels** | Every city your provider's local groups carry, A to Z, with the networks in each.  **OK** ticks a city; ◀ ▶ jump between letters.  The other cities' stations are hidden in every local group.  **Put them together in "Local channels"** (on) makes one group with your stations, by city and network, and **Then hide the network groups** (on) puts ABC Locals, CBS Locals and the rest out of the way so each station shows up once. |
| **Sports and pay-per-view** | [Event channels](#event-channels-pay-per-view-and-sports-passes): reading events from names, short names, Events by time, the time zone for names without one, and **Hide channels with nothing on**.  The next events are listed. |
| **Clear the clutter** | Divider groups, empty groups, the same channel more than once (keep Full HD, the default, or the best, HD, SD or every copy; the copies you hide are still backups: TiviBro switches to one by itself if yours stops working, and they stay under hold OK > Other versions), groups with words like PPV or Adult, and whether groups the provider adds later show up. |
| **Channel logos** | The [logo library](#channel-logos), its style, and which logo wins when the playlist and the guide both have one, with logos it found for your channels. |
| **Make it yours** | Accent colour, background, text size, start screen, turning on the last channel at launch, the preview in the list, and what happens when a channel stops working.  Colours change as you pick them. |
| **All set** | How many channels and groups you keep and hide, **Your choices** (one line per screen, like "Detroit in Local channels, network groups hidden": **OK** on one goes back to that screen, and **Skip to the summary** comes back), **Apply and start watching**, and a few things worth knowing. |

**Hidden, not gone.**  Everything the tour hides stays one press away in **Settings > Channels and groups**, and Apply saves a restore point first.  Good to know about what hiding does:

- **Extra copies** you hide are still backups: if the copy you keep stops working, TiviBro switches to one of them by itself.  See [Other versions of a channel](#other-versions-of-a-channel).
- **The other East or West feed** is not a backup, because it runs hours apart.
- **Network groups** hidden after **Put them together in "Local channels"** don't take your stations with them: they stay in Local channels.
- **Hidden groups and channels** leave the guide, All channels, Search and the watchlist (unless **All channels includes hidden groups** is on).
- **Channels with nothing on** come back by themselves when an event is named.

### Channel logos

Providers often leave logos out, or send some that no longer load.  The **logo library** fills them in from two open projects:

- **[iptv-org](https://github.com/iptv-org/database)**: each channel's own logo, for about 28,000 channels in 230 countries.
- **[tv-logos](https://github.com/tv-logo/tv-logos)**: one matching style made for dark screens, for about 7,700 channels.

TiviBro hosts no logo images.  It downloads a small list of where the logos are, only for the countries in your playlist, and each logo loads from its own project, the way your provider's logos do.  The list is kept on the TV for a week.

Channels are matched by their guide id ("CNN.us"), then by their cleaned name in their own country, then by a local station's call sign ("WXYZ") or, for a local station with no logo of its own, its network.  Event channels are left alone, since their names are games.  A provider logo that fails to load is replaced by the library's.

Turn it on in the set-up tour or in **Settings > Appearance and start screen > Logo library**:

- **Off**: only the playlist's and the guide's logos.
- **Fill in missing logos**: the library's logo for channels that have none.
- **Use for every channel it knows**: the library's logo everywhere it has one, so the whole list matches.

**Logo library style** picks **Matching set** (tv-logos) or **Original logos** (iptv-org); each falls back to the other.  On a real 9,800-channel playlist it found logos for about 3,600 channels.

### The remote

TiviBro works with the standard LG remote and the Magic Remote.  With the Magic Remote you can also point, click and scroll on every screen.  A legend at the bottom of each screen always tells you what the buttons do right there.

| Button | In the guide | Watching fullscreen |
|---|---|---|
| **OK** | First OK previews in the window, second goes fullscreen | Quick menu |
| **Hold OK** | Programme and channel options | Channel guide over the video |
| **Red** | Programme and channel options | Options for the channel |
| **Info** | Programme and channel options | Programme details |
| **Hold Info** | | Buffer info: how many seconds are banked and, when the player reports it, the stream's speed.  No Info button (most Magic Remotes): **OK**, then **Stream info** |
| **▲ ▼** | Move between channels | Channel up and down |
| **◀ ▶** | Move through time (◀ on "now" opens the groups) | ◀ rewind or last channel, ▶ info |
| **CH + / CH −** | Page up and down | Channel up and down |
| **⏪ ⏩** | Jump through time, into the past on catch-up channels | Skip in catch-up and movies |
| **▶ Play** / **❙❙ Pause** | Watch the previewed channel fullscreen | Play only plays and Pause only pauses; a single Play/Pause button switches between them.  Live TV can be paused too. |
| **Green** | Channels screen | Back to the TV guide |
| **Yellow** | Groups | Last channel |
| **Blue** | Settings | Audio track |
| **Back** | Back to now, then the groups | Back to the guide |
| **0 to 9** | Jump to a channel number (at once when no longer number starts with what you typed, otherwise after a short pause) | Jump to a channel number, the same way |

A few things work the way you would guess from other TV apps:

- Channel lists wrap around: **▲** on the first channel goes to the last, and **▼** on the last goes back to the first.  In the guide and in the group lists, **▼** on the last row goes back to the first, and **▲** on the first row stays where it is.  Holding an arrow stops at the end instead of flying past it.
- **▲** at the top of a list never opens the menu by surprise.  **◀** or **Back** does.
- **Leaving TiviBro:** press **Back** until the groups show on the home screen; it says **Press Back again to exit**, and one more **Back** closes the app.  The first screen works the same way.
- In a confirmation ("Delete this playlist?"), **Cancel** is selected first, so a stray **OK** never deletes anything.
- With the Magic Remote, clicking outside a pop-up closes it, clicking the progress bar in a movie or catch-up jumps there, and on Settings rows you can click the **‹ ›** arrows to change a value.

Buttons can be remapped under **Settings > Remote and buttons**, including keys TiviBro does not know yet (**Learn a button**).  **Button tester** shows what the TV sends for each key.  Every button, its default and the actions you can give it are listed in [Settings: Remote and buttons](#settings-remote-and-buttons).

### The guide

The guide is the home screen.  The channel you are on plays in the window at the top left, with the programme details beside it.

- **OK** on a channel previews it in the window.  **OK again** (or **▶ Play**) goes fullscreen.  If you prefer one press, set **Settings > Appearance and start screen > OK on a channel in the Guide** to *Fullscreen right away*.
- **▶** moves forward in time; **◀** moves back, and on "now" it opens the groups.
- Programmes you can replay carry a **↺** catch-up mark.
- **OK** on a future programme sets a reminder; on a past catch-up programme it replays it.
- **Hold OK** opens: Watch fullscreen, Play in the window, Earlier programmes, Remind me, Jump to now, and Channel options (favorite, copy to group, hide, rename).
- **No guide for this channel** (in **Hold OK** on a channel with no guide) is for a channel that will never have one: a radio stream, a camera, an event slot.  It stops saying "No guide information" and guide matching and public fill-in leave it out.  **Look for a guide again**, in the same place, undoes it, and **Settings > Guide > Channels with no guide wanted** lists them all.

**Not playing lately.**  A channel whose last two tries both failed, while the playlist's other channels play, gets a small amber tag: **not playing** in the guide's channel column, **not playing lately** on the Channels screen and in the phone app.  It's usually the provider: the channel may be off air or moved.  The tag goes the moment the channel plays again, and after three days on its own.

### Groups and the menu

Your groups sit right beside the guide: **Favorites** and your own groups first, then **All channels**, then your provider's groups.  Moving through the list updates the guide behind it.  **OK** or **▶** opens a group; **Hold OK** gives group options (rename, hide, sort, block).

**Recently added** lists channels that are new in your playlist since it last loaded, newest first, and they carry a **NEW** tag in the lists for a week (change it, or turn it off, under **Settings > Channels and groups > Recently added channels**).

Long lists: **CH + / −** moves a page at a time everywhere, and holding **▲** or **▼** speeds up the longer you hold it, so the end of a 5,000 channel group is a few seconds away.

**◀** or **Back** from the groups opens the menu: **Guide, Channels, Movies, Shows, My media, Search, Edit on phone, Settings**.  Movies and Shows appear only when your provider has them, and **My media** once you add a source.  **Edit on phone** isn't a screen: it turns on the [web editor](#turning-on-the-web-editor) and shows its QR code over whatever you're watching.

### Watching fullscreen

- **▲ ▼** or **CH + / CH −** change channel.  An info bar shows the channel, what's on now and next, and how far into the programme you are.
- **The screensaver waits.**  While a channel, a movie or episode, My media or catch-up plays, TiviBro asks the TV to hold off its screensaver (a homebrew one such as Aerial too).  When nothing plays, playback is paused, or you are in another app, the screensaver comes on as usual.  There is nothing to set.
- **▶** shows the full programme info.  **Yellow** jumps back to the last channel.  **Green** goes back to the TV guide.
- **Hold OK** (or **OK > Channel guide**) lays the channel guide over the video: the group's channels with what's on now.  **OK** on another channel switches to it and keeps the channel guide open so you can keep browsing; **OK** on the channel that's already playing closes it.  **◀ ▶** change group.
- **OK** opens the **quick menu**: Channel guide, From start (catch-up channels), Favorite, TV guide, Other versions (when the channel has them), Captions, Aspect, Sleep timer, Stream info and Reconnect.
- **Closed captions**: **Captions** in the quick menu turns them on or off for every channel, like a TV's CC button (also **Settings > Playback > Closed captions**, with a caption size).  They show in fullscreen when the channel sends them, which most US channels do.  Not with the TV player (Settings > Playback > Player engine); **Stream info** says whether this channel has captions.  Pick which buttons appear under **Settings > Remote and buttons > Quick menu buttons**.
- **Red** opens options for the channel: favorite, copy to group, other versions, watch from start, catch-up, rename, assign guide, hide, aspect and sleep timer.  **Hold OK** opens the channel guide over the video.
- **Stream info** (quick menu) shows the channel, how much is buffered, the player in use, the picture's resolution, the bitrate and catch-up, updating while it's open, with the buffer gauge at the top right.  Any button can open it: **Stream info and buffer** under **Settings > Remote and buttons**.  **Sleep timer** stops playback after 15 minutes to 2 hours.  **Aspect** sets the shape of the picture for this channel (or this movie or episode), or for all of them:
  - *Original*: the picture's own shape.  *Fill*: stretched to fill the screen.  *Zoom*: fills the screen, edges cropped.
  - *4:3*: shown 4:3 with bars at the sides.  *16:9*: for a 16:9 picture squashed into 4:3, which some channels send.
  - *21:9*: a 2.35:1 film with black bars above and below fills the screen; the sides are cropped.
  - *4:3 to 16:9*: a 4:3 picture, or a 4:3 picture with black bars at the sides, stretched out to fill a 16:9 screen.
  - Each channel, movie and episode remembers its own choice; **Use this aspect for everything** makes it the default.  The shapes past Zoom need the picture to be scaled by TiviBro; with the TV player (Stream info says which player is in use) some TVs keep the picture as it was, and the menu says so.

**Smooth playback.**  TiviBro picks a player per stream (MPEG-TS, the TV's own player, or HLS) and falls back automatically if one struggles.  It watches for frozen pictures and reconnects on its own, with a short wait that grows between tries.  On a channel that keeps running dry it holds a bigger cushion before playing again (shown as *Buffering NN%*), and lets it shrink back once the stream settles.  It also rides out providers that jump their timestamps, briefly refuse connections, or send corrupted data.  If a provider is slow or your Wi-Fi is weak, try **Settings > Playback > Buffer profile**, or switch the **Stream format** between MPEG-TS and HLS.

It tries hard never to just stop:

- **Wi-Fi drops:** it waits for the network ("No network.  Waiting for it to come back") without using up its retries, and reconnects the moment the TV is back online.
- **A channel keeps failing:** it tries the TV's own player and the provider's HLS version of the channel, then keeps retrying with a message on screen (every 6 seconds for the first few minutes, then every 15, for up to 20 minutes), so a channel that comes back after a provider hiccup recovers by itself.
- **A channel stops working for good:** if your playlist has the same channel more than once (in another quality, another group, or a copy you hid), TiviBro switches to the next version and tells you, steadiest quality first: Full HD, HD, unmarked, 4K, then SD.  If every version fails, the provider itself is probably down, so it goes back to the channel you chose and keeps retrying.  Set this under **Settings > Playback > When a channel stops working**: switch by itself, ask you, or just keep retrying.  More in [Other versions of a channel](#other-versions-of-a-channel).
- **A channel never starts:** after a couple of quick retries it says plainly whether the provider is refusing it or the TV can't decode it.  **OK** tries again.
- **Paused live TV:** a short pause carries on where you left off.  After a long pause TiviBro lets the connection go (the provider would drop it anyway) and **Play** brings you back to live.
- **Movies and catch-up** pick up from the same spot after a dropped connection, and when you leave TiviBro (Home button, another input) and come back.

**HDR, Dolby Vision and sound formats.**  TiviBro reads what each stream is before the picture starts: from the HLS playlist, from the MPEG-TS stream, or for movies from your provider's details.  The info panel shows it, for example *4K  HEVC  Dolby Vision 8  E-AC-3 5.1*, and movie details show it before you press play.

- **Dolby Vision** plays through the TV's own player, which switches the screen into Dolby Vision itself.
- **HDR10 and HLG** stay on TiviBro's built-in player by default, for its steadier buffering.  If HDR pictures look grey or washed out, set **Settings > Playback > HDR and Dolby Vision** to *TV player for all HDR*.
- **HEVC, AV1, AAC, Dolby Digital (AC-3) and Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC-3, Atmos)** play on webOS 6 and later TVs.  Anything the built-in player can't decode moves to the TV's player automatically.
- **DTS, Dolby TrueHD and FLAC surround:** some sound the TV can't play at all.  TiviBro converts it for you, see [Sound the TV can't play](#sound-the-tv-cant-play).
- **Settings > Updates, about and diagnostics > What this TV can play** lists every format and which player handles it on your TV.

### Sound the TV can't play

LG TVs from 2020, 2021 and 2022 (webOS 5, 6 and 22, including the C1 and C2) can't play **DTS** sound at all.  No LG TV plays **Dolby TrueHD**, and **FLAC** with more than two channels doesn't play either.  Many movie files have one of those as their only sound, so they play with no sound.

TiviBro checks the sound before a movie, episode or My media file starts:

- **Another audio track the TV can play**, in the same language (or your **Audio language**): TiviBro plays that one.  Nothing to convert.
- **Only sound the TV can't play:** the network helper converts it to **Dolby Digital (AC-3)** while you watch, and TiviBro says so once ("Converting DTS audio for this TV").  The picture isn't touched, only the sound.  5.1 stays 5.1; 7.1 becomes 5.1.  It works with a soundbar or receiver too, since every one of them takes Dolby Digital.
- **Seeking** in a converted movie takes a moment longer than usual: the conversion starts again from the new spot.  The time, the progress bar, where you left off and subtitles all keep the movie's own time.
- **Audio track** in the quick menu lists the movie's own tracks.  Picking another one the TV can't play converts that one instead.

The conversion needs the **audio converter**, a small program (about 2 MB) the network helper downloads once from TiviBro's releases on GitHub, checks and keeps on the TV's storage.  The first movie that needs it asks: *Get* downloads it and the movie starts with sound; *Not now* plays the movie as it is, with no sound, and says why.  You can get it or remove it any time in **Settings > Playback**.  It is turned off or on with **Settings > Playback > Convert audio the TV can't play**.

Live channels with sound the TV can't play are converted the same way, when the stream says what its sound is.

### Other versions of a channel

Many playlists carry the same channel several times: in Full HD, HD and SD, in two groups, or as a backup copy.  TiviBro recognises them by name and treats them as versions of one channel.

- Lists and the guide show a quality tag (**FHD**, **HD**, **SD**, **4K**) beside each copy.
- **Other versions…** (**Hold OK** on a channel, or the **Red** options while watching) lists the other copies with their quality and group, hidden copies included.  The quick menu has an **Other versions** button too.  **OK** switches to one.
- **When a channel stops working,** TiviBro switches to the next version by itself and says so ("Nova News 24 isn't working.  Switched to Nova News 24 (HD)"), steadiest quality first: Full HD, HD, unmarked, 4K, then SD.  This only happens once the player has run out of retries, so a short hiccup never moves you.  If every version fails, the provider itself is probably down: TiviBro goes back to the channel you chose and keeps retrying until it returns.
- **Hidden copies count.**  Hiding the extra copies (in the set-up tour, Smart clean-up or by hand) only takes them out of your lists: they are still used as backups and listed under Other versions.  Only a copy you **block** with the parental PIN is never switched to.
- **East and West feeds are not versions of each other.**  They run hours apart, so TiviBro never switches from one to the other by itself.
- **Settings > Playback > When a channel stops working** chooses between switching by itself, asking you first, or just retrying.

Pay-per-view, league passes (NFL, NBA, NHL, MLB), college sports and one-off match channels often have no guide at all. TiviBro reads those names:

- **Short channel names.**  The list shows the channel's own tag, "NCAAB 001", "PPV 03", "BigTen 01" or "ESPN+ 20", with the event on the line under it.
- **The event in the guide, at its time.**  Before it starts the guide says **Starts 7:30 PM: Kings vs Pelicans** (or **Starts Sat 9:00 PM** later in the week); during it, the matchup; for a few hours after, **Ended:**. - **Events by time,** a group at the top of the list with every event channel that is on now or starts in the next two weeks, soonest first: what's on next, across all your passes, in one place.  **Hold OK** on it to turn it off.

### My teams

Many providers have a group for each league's teams ("NFL TEAMS", "NHL TEAMS": "NHL | DETROIT RED WINGS") next to their league passes.  **My teams** gathers the games of the teams you follow into one group near the top of the guide:

1. **Games on now**, on every channel showing them: event channels ("USA NHL 02: WPG VS CAR"), league passes, sports networks and guide programmes ("NFL Football: Lions at Vikings").  A game's channels are listed together, so when one feed doesn't work the next is right there.
2. **Their next games**, starting within 6 hours (or 3, 12 or 24), soonest first, at the bottom.

Only games: the other team's own channel is left out (unless you follow both), channels in your own groups count even when their provider group is hidden, and a team's own channel is there while it shows a game, not when its guide says "Future events not found yet" or the next game is tomorrow night.  A show about one team (a pregame show, a team magazine) isn't a game.  Games a local station lists as "NFL Football", with the teams only in the description, count.

Between games the group stays, empty, and says when the next one is: "No games on now or in the next 6 hours.  Next: Detroit Red Wings at Florida Panthers, Tomorrow 7:00pm on NHL | DETROIT RED WINGS."

For now it covers the main North American leagues only: NFL, NBA, NHL, MLB, WNBA and MLS.  College, junior and minor league teams, and other countries' leagues, aren't in it yet.

Choose your teams in **Settings > Channels and groups > My teams**.  It lists the leagues in your playlist (NFL, NBA, NHL, MLB, WNBA, MLS); open one and switch on your teams.  Teams with their own channel say so.  Or **hold OK** on a team's channel and choose **Follow the Detroit Red Wings** (in the phone app: press and hold the channel).  Your teams are kept in the settings, so they stay when you switch playlists.

**Remind me before their games** (off at first) sets reminders for their games the way the [watchlist](#watchlist) does.  Unfollowing a team takes its reminders with it.

TiviBro knows the teams by their full names ("Detroit Red Wings"), by short names when the league is clear ("Red Wings" on an NHL channel, or "Heat at Magic"), by city when the league is named ("NHL: Winnipeg vs Carolina"), and by abbreviations in event channel names ("WPG VS CAR" in an NHL group).  So "Magic Mike" on a movie channel is never a game, and junior and minor teams with the same names ("Parkland Rangers U15", "Providence Bruins") are left out.  **Hold OK** on the group for **Choose teams…** or **Turn off My teams**.

### Movies and shows

If your provider offers video on demand, **Movies** and **Shows** appear in the menu.  With an Xtream Codes login they come from the provider's movie and series lists.  With an M3U link they come from the list itself: its films (links to `/movie/` or to a video file) go to **Movies**, and its episodes (links to `/series/`, or named like "Show S01 E02") are gathered into shows in **Shows**, by season, instead of filling the channel list.  The list's groups become the categories.  The network helper keeps them on the TV, so a list with hundreds of thousands of titles still fits.

- Categories on the left (**Continue watching**, **My list**, **All movies**, then your provider's), titles in the middle, details and poster on the right.
- **OK** plays a movie or opens a show's seasons and episodes.  **Hold OK** adds a title to **My list**.
- **Info** or **Hold OK** opens a title's options.  **CH + / CH −** page through long category lists.
- **Languages.**  Many providers put a language code in front of every title ("EN - ", "SE-4K - ", "[SE]") or on the category ("\|TR\| DIZI"). By default that's your TV's language plus titles with no code; a library in one language shows everything and has no Languages row.  A 4K tag becomes a 4K label.

- **Adult categories** (named Adults, XXX, 18+ and the like) are listed last and stay out of **All** and Search.  With a PIN set for blocked channels, they ask for it.

Keep pressing and each press jumps further (twice, four, then eight times as far); the info panel shows where you'll land and when the movie will end, and playback jumps there once you stop pressing.  **Back** cancels a jump you haven't landed yet.  **▲ ▼** skip 10 minutes.

Press **Green** on My media to rescan all saved sources for new or removed movies.  Press **Yellow** to fetch details and artwork for new movies or outdated language matches.  Yellow leaves up-to-date matches alone.  Wait for a rescan to finish before fetching details.

Use **Left** and **Right** to move between movies, and **Up** and **Down** to move between shelves.  **OK** plays or resumes the selected movie.  **Hold OK** or **Red** opens movie options: add or remove a favorite, start over, mark as watched, find or correct movie details, or see file information.  Favorites and your place in a movie stay with My media across playlists and restarts.

**Back** opens the standard main menu directly.  **Left** from the first movie does the same.  There is no separate source menu on the movie screen; all sources appear together and source management stays in Settings.

**Add media and preferences are in Settings > My media**, not in the movie shelves.  **Blue** from My media opens those settings directly.  Choose **Add media > Browse my network (DLNA / UPnP)** to find a home server, or **Add a WebDAV server** and enter its WebDAV address and login.  The phone app's **Settings > My media** does the same, with the typing on the phone.  Turn on media sharing on a home server first.  A playlist must already be set up.

**Movies and TV shows.**  A new source finds both.  A source added before alpha 61 stays movies only until you open it in **Settings > My media** and set **Content** to **Movies and TV shows** (or **TV shows only**), then rescan.

Shows open into seasons and episodes.  **Red (Options)** has **TV shows** and, when something needs a look, **Needs review**.  **Favorites** shows your favourite movies and shows; they stay in the **Movies** and **TV shows** lists too, unless you turn off **Favorites also in lists** in **Settings > My media**.

Name folders and files like this, and the rest takes care of itself:

```
Movies/
  Frozen (2013)/
    Frozen.2013.1080p.BluRay.x264.mkv
TV/
  Harbour/
    Season 1/
      Harbour.S01E01.mkv
      Episode 02.mkv
```

- **Movies:** a folder named "Title (Year)" gives the title and year, whatever the file is called.  Without one, the file name is used: dots and underscores become spaces, and it is cut at the quality words (1080p, BluRay, x265).
- **Episodes:** S01E02, 1x02, or "Episode 2" inside a "Season 1" folder give the season and episode.  Shows numbered by air date or by one running number get their own order.
- **Extras:** folders named Extras, Featurettes, Behind the Scenes, Deleted Scenes, Interviews, Trailers and the like aren't opened.  Files with trailer, teaser or featurette in the name are skipped, and so are videos under 100 MB.
- **Needs review:** a file TiviBro isn't sure about waits here.  Fix one file, or a whole folder (you see what changes before it's saved).  The web editor can do the same, with undo.
- **Deleted files:** a file you delete from the server leaves My media at the next complete rescan.  If it comes back within 30 days, it keeps its details, favorite and watched state.  A scan that couldn't read every folder, or found nothing, changes nothing.

**Show details.**  With a TMDB token saved, shows get their poster and details on their own, then season details and episode names, summaries and pictures for the seasons you have.  Only exact matches are picked automatically; **Find show details** on a show picks one yourself.  **Yellow** fetches details again.  TMDB gets show titles or IDs, season numbers and your language, with your token, and never a source's login or links.

**Episodes.**  Playback remembers where you stopped, and **Auto next episode** offers the next one.  For a file that holds several episodes, TiviBro uses its chapters, or you can set where each episode starts.

**Big libraries.**  A scan works in parts and carries on where it stopped, and the source's options have **Pause scan** and **Restart scan**.  A scan that fails or finds nothing keeps what you had.

A WebDAV source is a WebDAV folder, not a normal website or HTTP directory.  Services such as premiumize use a customer ID and API key as the login.  TiviBro supports both signed file links and servers that serve files directly with HTTP Basic authentication.  For a file that needs a login, the helper streams it to the player and handles seeking and subtitle reads.  Your login is sent only to the server you added.

For easier typing, open **Settings > My media > Edit on a computer** and connect to the web editor.  Its **My media** page lets you add WebDAV logins, rescan or remove sources, and change the shared metadata language.  Saved logins are never sent back to the editor.

In **Settings > My media**, choose a saved source for **Rescan**, **Fetch movie details**, or **Remove**.  Removing a source leaves your files alone.  A failed, incomplete or empty scan keeps the previous library.

**Movie details:** **Fetch movie details** looks up a source's movies on TMDB.  Create your own API key or API Read Access Token at [TMDB API settings](https://www.themoviedb.org/settings/api), then save it under **Settings > My media > Add TMDB API token**.  You can paste it in the web editor instead of typing with the remote.  The token is checked before saving, kept only on this TV, never sent back to the editor, and excluded from backups and problem reports.  Public builds contain no token.  Fetching details sends movie titles/years or saved movie IDs to TMDB.  Only an exact, unambiguous title/year match is saved automatically; use **Find movie details** or **Change movie details** in a movie's options to search and choose a match.  **Remove movie details** clears it.  Posters and backdrops load from image.tmdb.org.  Existing matches, including manually corrected matches, are refreshed by ID rather than searched again.  Details and poster addresses survive rescans and restarts.  With a token saved, movies without details or a poster are looked up on their own after each scan and when TiviBro starts; one TMDB can't match is left alone for a week (a manual fetch still tries).  **Test TMDB token** (in Settings and the web editor) asks TMDB whether the saved token still works.

**Subtitles for movies and shows:** the subtitle menu (**Red** > **Subtitles** while playing) has **Find subtitles…**, which looks on [OpenSubtitles.com](https://www.opensubtitles.com) for what's playing, in your **Subtitle languages** (several at once, Latin American Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese included).  The search is filled in from the title and year, or the show, season and episode; change the title when the subtitle sites know it by another name.  Pick one from the list (the most downloaded first, with its frame rate and whether it's for the deaf and hard of hearing) and it's on.
- It needs your own free API key: sign up at opensubtitles.com, then **Profile > API consumers > New consumer**, and paste the key in **Settings > Playback > Add your OpenSubtitles API key** (typing it from the phone app is easiest).  Without an account OpenSubtitles allows 5 downloads a day; **Sign in** with a free account for 20.  The key and login stay on the TV, out of backups.
- **Timing** moves any subtitle a little earlier or later (0.1 s or 1 s at a time).  **Timing > Frame rate…** fixes a subtitle made for a copy at another frame rate, which drifts further out of time as the film goes on: pick *Subtitle for 23.976 fps, video at 25 fps* and so on (23.976, 24 and 25 fps), and every line's time is scaled to match.  A subtitle that is in time, then early or late for a while, then in time again was made for another cut of the film: try another download.
- TiviBro draws subtitle lines itself, exactly the ones whose time is now, so a line can't stay stuck on screen after Timing, Line up or a seek moved it.
- **Line up with another track…** fixes a subtitle that drifts or starts at the wrong time by matching it to one that's already in time (a built-in one, or another download, in any language).  It finds the shift and a frame-rate difference (23.976, 24 or 25 fps) and says how much it moved.

Movies use the usual seek bar and subtitle menu.  Embedded SRT, ASS/SSA and WebVTT text subtitles in MKV files can appear when the file has a usable subtitle index, including larger indexes in movies with many languages.  Text subtitles in MP4 files appear too.  ASS/SSA and WebVTT show as plain text, without their custom fonts, positioning or animation.  Image subtitles such as VobSub or PGS and separate subtitle files from your server are not included yet.  **Find subtitles** can add a subtitle from OpenSubtitles even when the file has no supported embedded tracks.  **Back** hides the player controls first, then returns to the same movie.  Series, folder picking, Search and phone app browsing are not included yet.

Playback contacts servers you add and their playback servers.  Fetching details also contacts api.themoviedb.org with titles/years or saved movie IDs and the preferred language, never your source logins or playback links.  Previously saved Cinemeta posters may still load from images.metahub.space until those movies are refreshed.  Cloud playback links are resolved each time you play and are never saved.  After a long pause with an expired link, return to the shelves and resume.  The library uses the helper's persistent storage, so app restarts and updates keep it even when browser storage is full.  Source logins are outside playlist backups and problem reports; add the sources again after restoring on another TV.  Up to eight sources can be saved.

### Search

**Search** in the menu finds channels and programmes in the guide (now, later today, the coming days and on catch-up) as you type, and also your Movies and Shows.  Below the box, **Everything**, **On now**, **Coming up**, **Replays**, **Channels** and **Movies & Shows** narrow the results (**◀ ▶** on that row).  Each programme shows once, even when several copies of the channel carry it, with **Today**, **Tomorrow** or the day and its time.  **OK** watches, replays, opens a show's episodes, or sets a reminder (**OK** again cancels it); **Hold OK** gives channel options.  **Yellow** adds what you typed to your [watchlist](#watchlist).

Programmes that have what you typed only in their **description** come after the rest, a moment later, with the words around it: **lions** finds the local station whose "NFL Football" is "Minnesota Vikings at Detroit Lions".

Movies and shows are searchable without opening **Movies** or **Shows** first: Search reads the lists the TV keeps (or loads them once). So **batman** lists the Batman films before the 24/7 Batman channels, and **CNN** still finds the channel first.  Titles in languages you hid, and adult titles, don't show.  **OK** on a movie opens it in **Movies** and plays it, so **Back** lands on it there; on a show it opens its episodes.

### Reminders

**OK** (or **Hold OK > Remind me**) on a future programme in the guide or in search.  When it starts, TiviBro asks **Watch** or **Dismiss**, or switches by itself if you set **When a reminder starts** that way.  You can be reminded a few minutes early.

Reminders work with TiviBro closed too, as long as the TV is on: the TV shows its own alert over whatever you're watching, another app or an HDMI input included, and **Watch** opens TiviBro on the channel.  If it started a while ago and the channel has catch-up, TiviBro offers **Watch from the start**.  A reminder that came due while you were away is never lost: TiviBro asks when you come back, and one that already ended is offered from catch-up.  (Some TVs show a toast instead of an alert; select it to watch.)

Reminders are for someone using the TV.  When the screensaver, the Gallery or art screen, or Always Ready is showing, the alert waits and comes up once you're back (while the programme is still on); otherwise TiviBro asks the next time you open it.  Telling whether the screen is in use needs two permissions the TV only gives TiviBro on a rooted TV (installed with the Homebrew Channel or install.sh, which add them); elsewhere reminders show whatever is on screen.  With TiviBro closed, a rooted TV shows the alert with **Watch** and **Dismiss**; other TVs show a notification you can select to watch.  Inside TiviBro, a reminder waits if nobody has pressed a button for 10 minutes and nothing is playing.  Timing and behaviour are under [Settings: Reminders and watchlist](#settings-reminders-and-watchlist).

**Coming up.**  **Settings > Reminders and watchlist** lists every reminder still to come, soonest first, with its channel and the watchlist word or team that set it.  **OK** on one (**Cancel**) cancels it, after asking, and **Cancel all reminders** cancels every one.  The phone app's **Reminders** screen (a shortcut on the TV's card, and the bell on the Guide tab) and the web editor's [Reminders and watchlist](#reminders-and-watchlist) page show the same list, with the same Cancel and Cancel all.

### Watchlist

The watchlist looks for shows, teams and names in the guide for you: "Yankees", "Bluey", "Premier League", "Gordon Ramsay".  When one is coming up in the next 24 hours (or 6, 12 or 48), TiviBro sets a reminder, and says so.  Matching is by whole words in programme titles, so "Cars" never finds "Oscars".  A reminder you cancel doesn't come back, and a busy word keeps at most five reminders waiting at a time.

Add to it three ways: **Yellow** in Search (adds what you typed), **Hold OK > Add to watchlist** on a programme in the guide, or **Settings > Reminders and watchlist > Watchlist > Add a show, team or name…**.  That page lists your words, everything coming up (**OK** sets or cancels each reminder), and removes a word with the reminders it set.  The [web editor's](#reminders-and-watchlist) Reminders and watchlist page has the watchlist too, with a real keyboard.

The watchlist needs the guide to know what's on: with an XMLTV guide it sees every channel; with only the provider's per-channel guide, it sees the channels you've looked at.

### Parental controls

**Content filter**, at the top of **Settings > Parental controls**, takes channels out of TiviBro altogether, no PIN needed: they never show in the guide, any list, Search, the phone app or the web editor.

- **Hide adult channels** catches channels and groups named Adult, XXX, 18+, Porn, Erotic and the like, in several languages, and channels the provider marks as adult.  "Adult Swim" stays.  A new TV asks on the set-up tour's first screen, with **Yes** picked.
- **Add a word or call sign…** hides every channel and group with that whole word in its name: a city's affiliates by call sign, shopping channels, a sports league you never watch.
- While watching or in the guide, a channel's or group's options have **Hide with the content filter** (asked first).
- Each word, channel and group is listed there with **Remove**, and **Left out of this playlist** says how many channels and groups the filter takes out.  Nothing is deleted: remove an entry and the channels come back.

Set a PIN under **Settings > Parental controls**, then **Hold OK** on any channel or group and choose **Block channel** (or block a whole group from its options).  Decide whether the PIN guards blocked channels, Settings, or both, and whether it stays unlocked until TiviBro restarts.  **Lock now** locks it again straight away.  Blocked channels show **Locked** instead of their programme, and blocking the channel that's playing stops it.  All options: [Settings: Parental controls](#settings-parental-controls).

### Settings: Guide

Where the programme guide comes from, for the playlist in use, and the movies in it.  Each playlist's page under [Playlists](#settings-playlists) has the same guide rows for that playlist.

**How your guide is built.**  The page starts with the order TiviBro builds your guide in:

1. **Your provider's guide.**
2. **Your own guide sources**, the first one first.  They win over the provider's.
3. **Public fill-in**, only for channels nothing else covers: epg.pw, iptv-epg.org, and Pluto TV's own guide from i.mjh.nz for Pluto channels.
4. **Matching:** each channel is linked to a guide channel by its guide id, then its name.  A wrong one: **Hold OK > Assign guide**.

A channel that will never have a guide (a radio stream, a camera, an event slot): **Hold OK > No guide for this channel**.  It drops out of matching and public fill-in, and stops saying "No guide information".  The web editor's Guide page does many at once (**Ignore**).

With public guides on, if the provider guide fails, the guide status says it is checking public guides.  Once they supply current matching listings, it says **Provider guide unavailable; using public guides.**  If they supply none, it says so instead.  If the guide TiviBro already had is still there, it says it is **using the saved guide**.  The original error stays in the guide's details.

**Subtitles in MKV and MP4 movies and episodes.**  Open **Subtitles** from the player menu to choose a language or **Off**. Only the selected subtitle track is shown, even if another track becomes active while subtitles load.  ASS/SSA and WebVTT show as plain text.  My media uses the file's known type even when its signed playback link has no extension.  Files without supported tracks show an unavailable message; bitmap subtitles are not supported.  Some MKV files do not index their subtitle tracks and cannot expose them here.  **Find subtitles** offers another way to add them.

**MP4 subtitles** work for the usual text kind (called tx3g or mov_text), shown as plain text without their colours or positions.  Not included: MP4 files made for streaming in pieces (fragmented MP4), image subtitles, and closed captions inside MP4.  TiviBro makes extra requests on the same link as the movie: a few when the movie starts, to find the subtitles, and then, while a subtitle is on, small reads of the lines a little ahead of where you are as the movie plays (how often depends on the file).  Some providers refuse those reads: one that allows only one connection at a time, or one whose links stop taking new requests after a while.  The movie keeps playing either way.  TiviBro waits a little, asks the movie's link for a new connection and carries on; if the provider keeps refusing, it stops asking for this movie and **Subtitles** shows **Subtitles unavailable**.

### Turning on the web editor

1. On the TV, open **Edit on phone** in the side menu (◀ from the groups).  It turns the editor on and shows a QR code, the address and the password. ### Drafts and Send to TV

Every change is a **draft** until you press **Send to TV** (or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd>).  A bar at the bottom counts what hasn't reached the TV, and turns green when the TV confirms.

- **Review** lists every change in plain words before sending.  **Send automatically** sends as you go.
- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> undoes, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> redoes, even after sending.
- **Restore points** live on the TV.  It makes one before applying each batch (keeps the last 15), and you can make named ones before big changes.  Restoring one is itself undoable.
- **Your changes in this editor**, on the [Backups and restore points](#backups-and-restore-points) page, lists what hasn't reached the TV yet, this session's changes (each with **Go back to before this**), your draft history, and **Start over**.

**You can't lose unsent work.**  An hour of clean-up shouldn't vanish because of a stray click, a refresh or a closed tab, so the editor saves your drafts twice:

- **In this browser,** the moment you make a change.
- **On the TV,** a few seconds later (at least every 30 seconds while you keep working).  The bar at the bottom says when: *Draft saved on the TV at 8:41*.  If the TV can't be reached it says so, and the browser copy still holds your work.

Reload the page, close the tab, or open the editor on another computer or your phone, and it asks whether to pick up your unsent changes, whichever copy is newer.  **Keep working on them** brings everything back; **Throw them away** clears them, but a copy stays in the draft history.

**Draft history** (on **Backups and restore points**, under **Your changes in this editor**) keeps versions of your unsent work on the TV:

- and any you name with **Save a version** (for example *Before hiding the sports groups*).

**Open** goes back to a version and keeps working from there; it's an ordinary step, so **Undo** returns to where you were.  Versions that reached the TV are marked *sent to the TV*.  Each version holds only your edits (which groups and channels are hidden, renames, your groups and so on), not the channel list, so it's small: a few KB for a typical clean-up, about 200 KB for 12,000 changes on a 15,000-channel playlist.  The TV keeps up to 8 MB of draft history per playlist (40 automatic versions and 25 others), drops the oldest automatic ones first, and deletes a playlist's drafts with the playlist.

### Channels and groups

- The switch beside a group shows or hides it on the TV. Your groups move among your groups and always come first; provider groups move among provider groups.
- **✎** renames a group.  Renamed and edited groups are tagged so you can see what you changed.  To clean many group names at once, use [Channel and group names](#channel-and-group-names).
- Tick channels, then **Copy or move to group**.  Any group works as the destination, yours or the provider's.  Copy keeps the channel where it was; Move takes it out.
- **Order** sorts the open group on the TV: provider order, A to Z, Z to A, channel number, or **Your order**, for your groups and the provider's.  In Your order, drag **⠿** to move a channel, or use **⋯ > Move to the top, Move up one, Move down one, Move to the bottom** or **Move to position…** (on a phone, ⋯ is the only way).  Channels the provider adds to a group later go to the end.
- **👁** hides a channel everywhere; **★** adds it to Favorites.  Both work on many ticked channels at once.
- Your changes survive playlist refreshes.

### Smart clean-up

The same engine as the TV's [set-up tour](#the-set-up-tour), with more room: pick per channel, see every group, undo any step.  The tour's QR code opens this page signed in.

Every step shows what is in effect now, and re-reads it after each change, undo or redo, so coming back is updating your setup, not starting over.  Steps that hide things list what they hid, with a way to show it again.  It works as a checklist.  Each step says **✓ Done** (nothing left for it to do), **To do**, or **Skipped**, and the line at the top counts them.  **Skip** leaves a step as it is, and **Undo skip** brings it back.  Skips are saved on the TV, so another phone or computer sees them too.  The number on **Smart clean-up** in the menu is the steps still to do.

- **Adult groups** finds groups named Adult, XXX, 18+ and similar (not Adult Swim) and hides them in one press.  On the TV, Settings > Parental controls can also lock any group behind a PIN.
- **Keep only your countries** (with **Tick all** and **Untick all**, for keeping one or two out of many) works out each group's country and keeps the ones you tick.  It reads codes at the start of group names ("US \| News", "[UK] Sports"), country words ("Canada Sports", "France Cinema", "Latino / Mexico"), the code most of a group's channels start with ("ARG \| ..."), and the divider group a group sits under ("⫘⫘⫘ CAN ⫘⫘⫘" then "TSN +").  Groups with no country, like sports and event groups, stay unless you tick **Also hide groups with no country**.
- **Same channel, more than once** finds a channel in several qualities and keeps one (Full HD first is a good balance).  It opens on **To check**: only the channels where two copies have the same quality, so TiviBro couldn't tell which to keep.  **All** shows every set, one line each, with a button per copy, marked **Keep** on the one that stays and **Hide** on the rest (hidden copies stay backups): click a copy to keep that one instead, or **Keep all** to leave a set as it is.  Channels with different numbers in their names are never treated as copies, so "US (Peacock 002)" and "US (Peacock 014)" stay separate.  Type in **Find a channel** to narrow the list; **Hide extra copies** then works on just the channels found, or on all of them when the search is empty.
- **Hide by keyword** hides groups or channels with words like PPV or 24/7, with a preview.
- **Empty groups** hides groups with nothing left to watch.
- **East and West feeds** finds channels that come in both time zones ("HBO (EAST)" and "HBO (WEST)", "A&E" and "A&E (WEST)") and keeps the feed you pick, **Keep East** or **Keep West**.  The other copies are hidden in one step, and **Also hide groups named West** (or East) hides groups like "USA WEST" too.  Channels that only come in one feed are left alone.
- **Local channels: pick your city** reads the city out of every station in your local groups ("ABC Locals", "CBS Locals" and so on: ABC, CBS, NBC, FOX, CW, PBS, Telemundo, Univision, CBC, CTV, Global and more).  It starts with a search box: type your city (**Find your city: Chicago, Boston, Denver…**), tick it (or a few nearby, like Detroit and Lansing), and **Keep only these cities** hides every other city's stations in all the local groups at once.  Stations with no city in their name (national feeds such as Galavision or UniMás) are never put in your local group; **Leave stations with no city shown** keeps them visible where they are.  A city in brackets counts ("CW \| EAST (PHILLY)" is Philadelphia).  Coming back later, your earlier pick is ticked, so you are updating it, not starting over.  **Also put them together in one group called** ("Local channels", or a name of your own) copies the stations you keep into one group, by city and network, and **Then hide the network groups** hides ABC Locals, CBS Locals and the rest, so each station shows up once.  Running it again adds to the same group.
- **Divider groups** hides groups like "⫘⫘⫘ UK ⫘⫘⫘" or "===== SPORTS =====" that only split the provider's list into sections.
- **Channels with nothing on** is the same switch as **Hide channels with nothing on** on the TV: event channels named "Off Air", "Offline" or "No Event Scheduled", empty slots, and events that ended hours ago stay out of the lists while they say so.
- **Groups of old events**: event groups where every event ended days ago (the provider stopped updating them).  Hide them in one go; they stay hidden until you show them again.

Everything here only hides, never deletes, and each action is one undo step.  Taking a word off many group names at once (**Shorter group names**) is now on [Channel and group names](#channel-and-group-names).

### Channel and group names

Rules strip clutter from every channel and group name at once: country prefixes like `US |`, quality tags like `FHD`, brackets and more.  Your provider's names are untouched, so switching a rule off brings the original back.  Names you set by hand stay.  The same rules are on the TV under **Settings > Channel and group names** ([details](#settings-channel-and-group-names)).

### The programme guide

The editor's **Guide** page starts with **How the TV builds your guide**, the same order as on the TV:

1. **Your provider's guide**, loaded with your playlist and again on the reload schedule you set in Playlists.
2. **Your own guide sources**, which come first when both cover a channel.
3. **Public fill-in**, for channels that still have nothing once 1 and 2 have loaded: epg.pw, iptv-epg.org, and Pluto TV's own guide for Pluto channels.  At most twice a day.
4. **Matching**: most channels find their guide by themselves; step 4 lists the rest, to match or ignore.

The **Jump to** links under it go to each step, and to **Reminders and watchlist**.

**Where it comes from.**  For Xtream providers:

- **Per channel** (recommended for most): the TV asks for each channel's next shows as you browse.  Fast and light.
- **Full XMLTV file**: the TV downloads your provider's whole guide.  Days ahead and manual matching, but files are often 20 to 200 MB.  Live progress (speed, percent, time left) shows in the editor and in the corner of the TV.
- **Off**: only your own sources.

M3U playlists can use the guide address inside the playlist.

**Extra sources.**  Add any `.xml` or `.xml.gz` guide.  Zipped guides work on every TV: the network helper unzips them as they download, and it also unzips provider guides that arrive gzipped.  **Recommended free guides** lists community guides by region (epgshare01, iptv-epg.org, i.mjh.nz for Pluto, Samsung TV Plus, Plex and Roku, and more), each with a one-click **Test**.  The first source in the list wins when two cover the same channel, and **Offset** fixes a guide that is an hour off.

**Public guide fill-in.**  Step 3 on the Guide page (on by default).  After your sources and your provider's guide have loaded, the TV lists the channels that still have nothing and looks them up in two free, community-run guides: **epg.pw** (20 countries, lighter files, tried first) and **iptv-epg.org** (70 countries, bigger and more complete).  Channels match by guide id, a US local station's call sign or the name, and only in their own country's guide when TiviBro can tell the country.  Pluto TV channels (guide ids like "US_5ca5…") are also looked up in Pluto's own guide from i.mjh.nz, by id, when there are three or more of them with nothing.  What it finds never replaces a guide you already have.  The card shows how many channels were filled in from each, lets you turn it off, and lets you pick up to eight countries instead of **Automatic** (the countries of the channels that need a guide, up to four).  The TV fetches the files itself, at most twice a day, in the background once your own guide is on screen; iptv-epg.org needs the network helper (Settings > Playback > Connection: Automatic).  Programmes from a public guide say "public guide" on the TV, and a wrong match is fixed like any other: Smart match or **Hold OK > Assign guide**.

**Matching channels to the guide** (step 4).  The tabs sort your channels: **Needs a guide**, **Guide is empty**, **Provider only**, **Matched by you**, **Matched on its own**, **Waiting for the TV**, **All** and **Ignored**.  **Smart match** fixes channels showing "No guide information".  It ignores clutter, understands country codes, East and West feeds, numbered channels and US call signs, then shows its best guess with a confidence score.  You approve or decline each one, and nothing changes until you press **Apply**.

**Ignore.**  Radio stations, cameras and 24/7 loops never have a guide.  **Ignore** on a row, or tick several and press **Ignore for the guide**, and they move to the **Ignored** tab: they leave every other tab, the counts and Smart match, public fill-in skips them, and the TV stops saying "No guide information" for them.  **Look for a guide again**, in the Ignored tab, undoes it.  On the TV it's **Hold OK > No guide for this channel** (and **Look for a guide again**), and **Settings > Guide > Channels with no guide wanted** lists them.

### Backups and restore points

One page for both safety nets, and for your unsent changes (**Your changes in this editor**, see [Drafts and Send to TV](#drafts-and-send-to-tv)).

**Backup file.**  A backup is one file with everything: settings, every playlist **with its login**, each playlist's groups, favorites, names and guide matches, and its restore points.  Channel lists and guides aren't in it; they reload from your provider.

**Always locked.**  You choose a passphrase (or press **Suggest one**).  The TV makes the backup and locks it with AES-256-GCM before it leaves the TV, so the file on your computer is useless without the passphrase.  Nobody can recover a forgotten passphrase, so keep it in a password manager.  The phone app's **Save a file** makes the same kind of file.

**Restoring, even on a brand-new TV:**

1. Install TiviBro and open it.  Choose **Set up or restore from a computer**.  (On a TV already set up: **Settings > Phone app and web editor**.)
2. Open the address shown on the TV and type the password.
3. **Restore a backup**, drop the file, type the passphrase and press **Unlock**.  The editor lists the playlists inside so you know it's the right file.
4. **Restore to the TV.**  TiviBro restarts with everything back, and the editor reconnects by itself.

**Restore points on the TV.**  Snapshots of this playlist's groups, channels, names and guide matches.  The TV makes one by itself before it takes changes from this editor; **Create restore point** makes your own before a big clean-up.  Restoring one makes another first, so a restore can be undone too.  The TV's **Settings > Backups and restore points** and the phone app's Backups and restore points screen list the same restore points.

Inside the phone app, the backup file and restore point parts hand over to the app's own Backups and restore points screen; your unsent changes stay here.

## Troubleshooting

**Settings > Updates, about and diagnostics > Run connection test** is the best first step when something doesn't work: it checks each part in turn and says what failed.

<p align="center">
  <a href="https://tivibro.app/view/#tv-set-diag-test"><img src="screenshots/tv-set-diag-test.png" width="60%" alt="Connection test"></a>
</p>

<a name="what-is-the-network-helper"></a>**What is the network helper?**  The **network helper** is a part of TiviBro that runs in the background on the TV.  It talks to your provider for the app: it fetches the channel list, the guide and the streams, sends the User-Agent your provider wants, unzips guides, and copes with servers that don't follow the rules.  webOS won't let an app talk to most IPTV servers directly, so TiviBro needs it.  It starts with TiviBro, runs only on the TV, and contacts your own provider, media sources you add and their playback servers (and, if you turn them on, the logo projects).

You'll see it in three places: **Connection** on the first screen and in **Settings > Playback** (leave it on **Automatic**), **Network helper** in Settings > Playback and in the diagnostics status (it should say *running*), and the connection test.  If it says *not running*, restart TiviBro; if that doesn't help, **Settings > Updates, about and diagnostics > View network helper log** shows why.

| Problem | Try this |
|---|---|
| A channel won't start or keeps buffering | **OK** on the error message, or **Reconnect** in the quick menu.  Then try **Stream format** HLS instead of MPEG-TS, or a bigger **Buffer profile**. |
| Every channel fails | **Settings > Updates, about and diagnostics > Run connection test**.  It checks the [network helper](#what-is-the-network-helper), your login, the channel list, a stream and the guide, step by step. |
| "No guide information" | Check that **Public guide fill-in** is on (Settings > Guide), and that its countries include the channel's.  Then use **Smart match** in the web editor, or **Hold OK > Assign guide** on the TV.  Or add an extra guide source for your region.  A channel that will never have a guide: **Hold OK > No guide for this channel** (or **Ignore** in the web editor). |
| Guide times are off by an hour | **Provider guide time offset** in Settings, or **Offset** on an extra source in the editor. |
| TiviBro closes after reloading a very big playlist (100,000 channels or more) | A very big list is a lot for a TV to hold, and a reload is the hardest moment.  Set **Settings > Playlists > the playlist > Reload channel list** to **Only when I ask** and reload when you're not watching, or ask your provider for a smaller list.  TiviBro reloads one list at a time, lets the network helper save it, looks for at most 30,000 channels in the public guides and sends the phone app at most 30,000.  If TiviBro closes twice in a row while loading a playlist, it sets that playlist to **Only when I ask** itself and says so; when nothing of that list is kept yet, the next start asks whether to try it again, change the connection or use another playlist instead of loading it a third time. |
| An M3U list's films and series aren't in Movies and Shows | They come in when the network helper reads the list, so **Reload channels now** once after updating.  Films need a link to `/movie/` or to a video file (`.mp4`, `.mkv` and the like), and episodes a link to `/series/` or a name like "Show S01 E02".  If TiviBro says a list is too big for this TV, ask your provider for a smaller one. |
| A playlist takes forever to load | **Back** on the loading screen offers to stop: change the connection, use another playlist or add one.  During a reload, **Settings > Playlists > the playlist > Stop reloading** stops it and keeps the channels you had. |
| Most channels won't play, with errors like 404 or 522 | When five or more channels fail with errors from the provider and next to none play, TiviBro says so (once a day).  The login can work and the account be active while the list itself is out of date, or its channels only work from another country (with a VPN).  **Reload the channel list**, or ask your provider. |
| The guide seems stuck loading | Big XMLTV files take minutes on a TV; watch the progress bar.  If it stays at 0, **Test** the address. |
| The web editor says it can't reach the TV | TiviBro was closed or the TV went to sleep.  Open TiviBro; the editor reconnects and your drafts are safe. |
| Changes don't show on the TV | Check the bar at the bottom of the editor.  If it still counts changes, press **Send to TV**. |
| Wrong editor password | It's on the TV under **Settings > Phone app and web editor**.  After five wrong tries, wait a minute. |
| HDR looks grey or washed out | **Settings > Playback > HDR and Dolby Vision > TV player for all HDR**. |
| A movie plays with no sound | It may only have DTS, Dolby TrueHD or FLAC surround sound, which the TV can't play.  Check that **Settings > Playback > Convert audio the TV can't play** is *Automatic* and that **Audio converter** says *Ready* (**Get the converter now** if not).  See [Sound the TV can't play](#sound-the-tv-cant-play). |
| Dolby Vision shows odd green or purple colours | Make sure **HDR and Dolby Vision** isn't set to *Built-in player*. |
| A remote button does nothing | **Settings > Remote and buttons > Button tester**, then **Learn a button**. |
| Something crashed, or TiviBro closed by itself | The next time it starts, TiviBro asks whether to report it and says what it was doing (loading which guide, playing which channel, how much memory it used).  **Send a report now** sends it from the TV straight away; **Report with my phone** shows the code for your phone so you can add what you were doing.  The report carries the log of the session that closed.  The TV turning off or restarting, and updates, don't count as a crash.  Test builds also have **Report a problem** at the bottom of the menu, and it is always in **Settings > Updates, about and diagnostics**.  **View app log with earlier sessions** shows the log on the TV.  Logs mask usernames and passwords.  When the developer answers your report, TiviBro shows the reply; if it still happens, **Still happening** sends a fresh log to the same report. |
